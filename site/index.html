Abstract

Synthetic generation of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) sessions is challenged by two competing demands: adhering to strict therapeutic structure while modeling the resistant, unpredictable behavior of real patients. Existing script-based methods fail to capture dynamic therapeutic interactions, while multi-agent approaches struggle to adhere to CBT's sequential structure; both suffer from sycophancy, producing overly compliant patients that misrepresent real clinical settings. In this work we introduce ODRA, a novel framework for synthesizing therapy dialogues through a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) strategy grounded in CBT guidelines. ODRA further incorporates a resistance orchestrator to solve patient sycophancy, which employs steering techniques to elicit behaviors aligned with their resistance level. Automated and expert evaluations show that ODRA significantly outperforms existing methods across therapeutic skills, CBT alignment, and patient behavioral fidelity, with licensed psychologists preferring ODRA sessions across 12 of 13 clinical metrics. Furthermore, models fine-tuned on our dataset demonstrate superior therapeutic performance against both cooperative and resistant patients, validating that explicit resistance modeling in synthetic training data directly translates to downstream clinical robustness.

What ODRA does differently

Three gaps in existing synthetic-CBT methods, and what we do about each.

Structural accuracy

Prior work borrows isolated CBT techniques. The CBT Aligner drives the whole session through the five stages of the foundational CBT protocol, with state carried across them. For instance, the cognitive models found in Conceptualization select the techniques used in CBT Work.

Patients that push back

Baselines told to be "negative" still show overcompliance. The Resistance Orchestrator tracks a continuous resistance scalar and steers each utterance to it, lifting resistance alignment from 0.70–1.19 to 1.96 out of 2.

Reasoning you can audit

Every utterance is preceded by explicit CoT steps that are captured, not discarded. 18,496 traces, rated 4.84/5 for contextual adherence by licensed psychologists. Read them turn by turn in the explorer below.

How it works

Two components sit on top of a turn-by-turn dialogue loop. The CBT Aligner governs the therapist's progression through the protocol; the Resistance Orchestrator governs how the patient feels about it, and how well the therapist reads that.

The CBT Aligner — five stages, in order

Standard CBT runs over many sessions; we adapt it to single-session synthesis. The therapist cannot advance until the Aligner's stage-completion check passes, and any Therapy-Interfering Behavior suspends the progression until it is addressed.

  1. 01
    Opening

    Identify mood and the primary presenting problem.

  2. 02
    Cognitive Conceptualization

    Build cognitive models; infer the likely distortion behind each.

  3. 03
    CBT Work

    Apply techniques targeted at the distortions identified.

  4. 04
    Homework

    Propose and agree consolidation tasks.

  5. 05
    Closing

    Summarize insights, plan ahead, end the session.

The Resistance Orchestrator

Resistance is a continuous variable r ∈ [0, 1], from fully cooperative to highly resistant. Each turn the patient model infers a shift ΔLLM, which is bounded to [−0.10, +0.15]. A mean-reverting term then pulls resistance back toward the profile's baseline, modelling emotional homeostasis:

rt+1 = rt + ΔLLM − α(rt − rbase)

with α = 0.15. The therapist separately maintains an estimate of that hidden state from what the patient says and adapts its replies to it. Both quantities are plotted live in the explorer below.

The Behavioral Profiler

Rather than a static "act negative" instruction, the profiler re-selects a steering prompt every turn to match the patient's current resistance band. Each prompt carries general rules, fine-grained calibration to the exact scalar, guardrails against drifting out of band, and in-context demonstrations.

The core problem

Same patient, same instruction, three methods

In this section we compare the same patient profile and attitude across three methods: CACTUS, MAGNeT, and ODRA. All three are instructed to produce a negative patient, but the results are significantly different. While CACTUS and MAGNeT produce compliant, sycophantic patients, ODRA produces a resistant patient that actively challenges the therapist.

Results

Automated LLM-as-a-judge evaluation (GPT-4o) plus a blind ranking study with three licensed psychologists over 50 sample sets — 300 session evaluations and 540 reasoning traces.

Reading the CTRS table. Full ODRA scores are lower than some baselines on counseling metrics, and that is the expected result since its patients are actively obstructing the session. The fair comparison against baselines, which all face compliant patients, is ODRA-NR (resistance orchestrator off). Full ODRA's value shows up under the resistant conditions in the fine-tuning table, and in behavioral alignment.

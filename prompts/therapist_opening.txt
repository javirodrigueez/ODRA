## Knowledge Base – Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)
CBT is a structured, goal-oriented therapy approach focused on the link between thoughts (cognitions), feelings (emotions), and actions (behaviors). The goal is to identify and change unhelpful patterns to improve emotional regulation and develop personal coping strategies.
### CBT Principles
* The Cognitive Model: Problems are maintained by the way we interpret situations. It is not the event itself that causes distress, but the meaning we assign to it.
* Collaborative Empiricism: The therapist and client work as a team to "test" the reality of the client's thoughts.
* Structure and Goal-Orientation: Sessions follow a specific agenda, focusing on solving current problems and teaching skills the client can use independently.
* Guided Discovery: Instead of telling the client they are wrong, the therapist uses questioning to help the client discover their own cognitive distortions.

### CBT Techniques
CBT focuses on "Restructuring." This involves identifying and challenging unhelpful thoughts and beliefs, and replacing them with more balanced, realistic ones. Here are some CBT techniques that can be used in therapy sessions:
* Socratic Questioning: The therapist asks a series of focused questions to help the client evaluate the evidence for their thoughts (e.g., "What is the evidence that this thought is true?").
* Identify Automatic Negative Thoughts (ANTs): Helping the client catch the "knee-jerk" negative thoughts that occur immediately after a trigger.
* Labeling Distortions: Teaching the client to identify patterns like "All-or-Nothing Thinking," "Catastrophizing," or "Mind Reading."
* Behavioral Activation: Collaboratively creating a plan to engage in activities that increase a sense of mastery or pleasure to counter avoidance.
* Functional Analysis: Examining the "ABC" chain: Antecedent (Trigger) → Belief (Thought) → Consequence (Feeling/Behavior).
* Cognitive Restructuring: Helping the client generate more balanced, realistic alternative thoughts to replace distorted ones.
* Homework/Action Plans: Assigning small, tangible tasks between sessions to practice new skills in the real world.

# Definition
CBT is a problem-focused, action-oriented approach aimed at alleviating distress by modifying maladaptive thinking and behavior patterns. It focuses on the interplay between thoughts, emotions, and behaviors (situations trigger thoughts, which in turn influence emotions and behaviors). Within CBT, thoughts are considered to play a central role in mental health. 

# How disorders are addressed?
Psychological disorders are understood to be maintained by unhelpful cognitive distortions (e.g. negative beliefs, biased thoughts) and behaviors, and improvement comes from learning new coping skills and ways of processing information (modify maladaptive thinking). Clients are taught to identify and challenge “automatic thoughts” and underlying core beliefs or schemas that may be distorted or unhelpful.

# Key concepts
* Cognitive model: The triangle of thoughts, emotions, and behaviors—each influencing the others. For example, a change in thinking can lead to a change in mood and behavior.
* Cognitive distortions: Systematic errors in thinking (e.g., "I must be perfect or I'm a failure") that CBT works to correct. Therapists help clients gather evidence and reframe these thoughts.
* Collaborative empiricism: Therapist and client work together to test beliefs like scientists examining evidence, rather than the therapist being an all-knowing authority.
* Behavioral activation and exposure: Encouraging engagement in positive activities or gradual exposure to feared situations to break cycles of avoidance and improve mood.
* Skills and homework: Clients practice coping skills (relaxation, problem-solving, etc.) and complete homework between sessions, reflecting CBT's emphasis on skill-building and real-world practice.

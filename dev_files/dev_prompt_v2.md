<mi_topics>
* **Health** Explore how the client's behavior impacts their physical and mental well-being, potentially leading to chronic health issues. Discuss the benefits of the **counseling goal**, which can improve overall quality of life and promote better long-term health outcomes.

* **Economy** Examine how the client's behavior affects their financial situation, such as through reduced productivity, increased absenteeism, or poor financial management. Contrast this with how the **counseling goal** helps improve economic stability and workplace performance.

* **Interpersonal Relationships** Analyze how the client's behavior affects personal relationships, leading to family strain or parenting challenges. Discuss how the **counseling goal** strengthens these bonds and fosters a healthier, more supportive family dynamic.

* **Law** Evaluate how the client's behavior increases legal risks, such as arrests, imprisonment, or traffic violations. Highlight how the **counseling goal** helps mitigate legal troubles and promotes a more responsible, law-abiding approach to life.

* **Education** Investigate how the client's behavior interferes with educational progress, leading to poor attendance, suspension, or missed academic opportunities. Discuss how the **counseling goal** fosters better academic performance and long-term success.
</mi_topics>

<mi_techniques>
### **Communication Categories**
* **Advise** Give advice, make a suggestion, or offer a solution or possible action.  
    *Example: "Consider starting with small, manageable changes like taking a short walk daily."*
* **Affirm** Say something positive or complimentary to the client to build self-efficacy.  
    *Example: "You did well by seeking help."*
* **Direct** Give an order, command, or direction using imperative language.  
    *Example: "You've got to stop drinking."*
* **Emphasize Control** Directly acknowledge or emphasize the client's freedom of choice, autonomy, or personal responsibility.  
    *Example: "It's up to you to decide whether to drink."*
* **Facilitate** Provide simple utterances that function as "keep going" acknowledgments.  
    *Example: "Tell me more about that."*
* **Inform** Give information, explain a concept, or provide specific feedback.  
    *Example: "This is a hormone that helps your body utilize sugar."*
### **Questioning Techniques**
* **Closed Question** Ask a question to gather specific facts (yes/no, numbers, etc.).  
    *Example: "Did you use heroin this week?"*
* **Open Question** Ask a question that allows for latitude and encourages the client to tell their story.  
    *Example: "Can you tell me more about your drinking habits?"*
### **Intervention & Feedback**
* **Raise Concern** Point out a possible problem with a client's goal, plan, or intention.  
    *Example: "What do you think about my plan?"*
* **Confront** Directly disagree, argue, judge, label, or question the client's honesty.  
    *Example: "What makes you think that you can get away with it?"*
* **Simple Reflection** Restate or repeat the client's words to show shallow understanding without adding new meaning.  
    *Example: "You don't want to do that."*
* **Complex Reflection** Reflect back content while adding depth or inferring a deeper meaning.  
    *Example: "That's where you drew the line."*
* **Reframe** Suggest a different meaning for an experience, placing it in a new light.  
    *Example: "Maybe this setback is actually a sign that you're ready for change."*
* **Support** Provide generally supportive or understanding comments (not coded as Affirm or Reflect).  
    *Example: "That must have been difficult for you."*
* **Warn** Provide a warning or threat regarding negative consequences.  
    *Example: "You could go blind if you don't manage your blood sugar levels."*
* **Structure** Explain the session flow or transition between topics to help the client anticipate what is next.  
    *Example: "First, let's discuss your drinking, and then we can explore other issues."*
</mi_techniques>

<cbt_techniques>
* **Efficiency Evaluation**: Assists individuals in evaluating the usefulness of their thoughts or beliefs, analyzing how practical or detrimental they are in real-life situations.
* **Pie Chart Technique**: Used for individuals experiencing excessive self-blame or responsibility, visually representing the contribution of various factors to a specific event or outcome.
* **Alternative Perspective**: Involves asking clients how others might think in similar situations, encouraging consideration of different interpretations.
* **Decatastrophizing**: Aims to reduce the tendency to imagine the worst-case scenario by evaluating the actual likelihood of the feared outcome and preparing for coping strategies.
* **Pros and Cons Analysis**: Analyzes the advantages and disadvantages of specific thoughts or beliefs, fostering a more balanced evaluation.
* **Evidence-Based Questioning**: Guides clients to find evidence supporting or contradicting their thoughts, promoting a more evidence-based approach to thinking.
* **Reality Testing**: Explores how well clients' thoughts align with reality, helping them distinguish between thoughts and actual experiences.
* **Continuum Technique**: Positions clients' experiences between two extreme situations, encouraging a more nuanced evaluation of situations.
* **Changing Rules to Wishes**: Replaces strict rules or arbitrary attitudes with realistic hopes or wishes.
* **Behavior Experiment**: Involves trying out new behaviors in specific situations to challenge and modify negative beliefs.
* **Problem-Solving Skills Training**: Learning systematic methods for resolving problem situations. This involves identifying problems, finding possible solutions, and implementing those solutions.
* **Systematic Exposure**: Gradual exposure to situations that cause fear or anxiety, allowing individuals to experience anxiety while learning how to manage it.
* **Cognitive Restructuring**: The process of identifying, evaluating, and responding to adaptive thoughts and beliefs using Socratic questioning and evidence-gathering.
* **Role Playing**: A session-based simulation of real-life interactions to practice new skills or gain a different perspective on a belief.
* **Activity Scheduling**: Planning and engaging in activities that are enjoyable or provide a sense of accomplishment to counteract negative thoughts and improve mood.
</cbt_techniques>

<cognitive_distortions>
* **Mental Filtering**: This thinking style involves a 'filtering in' and 'filtering out' process - a sort of 'tunnel vision,' focusing on only one part of a situation and ignoring the rest. Usually this means looking at the negative parts of a situation and forgetting the positive parts.
* **Jumping to Conclusions: Fortune-Telling**: Assuming that we know what is going to happen in the future.
* **Jumping to Conclusions: Mind-Reading**: Assuming that we know what someone else is thinking.
* **Personalization**: Blaming yourself for everything that goes wrong or could go wrong, even when you may only be partly responsible or not responsible at all. Taking 100% responsibility for the occurrence of external events.
* **Catastrophizing**: Occurs when we 'blow things out of proportion', viewing the situation as terrible, awful, dreadful, and horrible, even though the reality is that the problem itself is quite small.
* **Black-and-White / All-or-Nothing Thinking**: Seeing only one extreme or the other. You are either wrong or right, good or bad. There are no in-betweens or shades of gray.
* **Should Statements**: Putting unreasonable demands or pressure on yourself and others by saying 'I should...' or 'I must...', creating unrealistic expectations.
* **Overgeneralization**: Taking one instance in the past or present, and imposing it on all current or future situations. Using words like 'always', 'everyone', or 'never'.
* **Labeling and Mislabeling**: Making global statements about yourself or others based on behaviour in specific situations, even though there are many more examples that aren't consistent with that label.
* **Discounting the Positive**: Magnifying the positive attributes of other people and minimizing your own positive attributes, explaining away your own positive characteristics.
</cognitive_distortions>

<core_beliefs>
Core beliefs fall into three categories (Beck, 1995):
* **Helplessness**: Beliefs related to personal incompetence or powerlessness (e.g., "I am incompetent", "I am powerless", "I can't handle things", "I am weak", "I am a failure").
* **Unlovability**: Beliefs related to being unworthy of love or connection (e.g., "I am unlovable", "I am unwanted", "I will always be rejected", "I am bound to be alone", "I am not good enough to be loved").
* **Worthlessness**: Beliefs related to being defective or without value (e.g., "I am worthless", "I am defective", "I am useless", "I don't matter", "I am a burden").
</core_beliefs>

# PROBLEM
Right now the approach for generating the session is simple, just: 
Opening utterances -> standard utt. -> closing.

# SOLUTION
We need to create a complex, more structured therapy session. We are going to use as knowledge base Beck's CBT theory to develop therapy sessions according to Beck's methodology. The therapy should be divided in the next stages: Opening (opening), Identification of cognitive distortions (id_distortions), CBT technique (technique), Homework assignment (hw) and Closing (closing). The stages are clearly explained next:
1. Opening: First utterances where the therapist welcomes the patient and leaves space for the patient to express their problems.
2. Id. distortions: Agents are engaged on a multi-turn conversation where the objective of the therapist is to extract some items from the Beck's cognitive model, for then identifying cognitive distortions of the patient.
3. CBT Technique: Taking in mind the distortions identified and the conversation context, the therapist selects one technique to apply from <cbt_techniques>. The therapist will begin the development of the technique plan and both agents will be engaged on the activity.
4. Homework: Based on the complete conversation, the therapist assigns homework to the patient.
5. Closing: The therapist closes the session making a final summary, showing the next session plan and requesting feedback.

Particularly, the solution needs a strong mechanism to handle patient resistance. This will be mainly composed by a continuous resistance variable in the patient (values 0.0-1.0). Patient resistance is grounded on Transtheoretical Model (TTM), specifically with Precontemplation, Contemplation and Action states. During the CBT flow, we will put alternative paths that will be followed if the therapist detects resistance >0.75 (precontemplation) and resistance >0.5 (contemplation).

# STATE TRANSITION DIAGRAM

The session follows this state machine. The primary (happy-path) flow is:

    OPENING ──► ID_DISTORTIONS ──► CBT_TECHNIQUE ──► HOMEWORK ──► CLOSING

After OPENING completes, the path depends on the therapist's predicted resistance (R):
* R < 0.5 (Action) ──► ID_DISTORTIONS (begin CBT flow)
* 0.5 < R ≤ 0.75 (Contemplation) ──► MI_TECHNIQUES ──► ID_DISTORTIONS ──► ...
* R > 0.75 (Precontemplation) ──► MI_TOPIC_EXPLORATION ──► MI_TECHNIQUES ──► ID_DISTORTIONS ──► ...

During any CBT stage (id_distortions, cbt_technique, homework):
* If R enters Contemplation (0.5, 0.75] ──► CBT interrupted ──► MI_TECHNIQUES ──► (resume CBT stage)
* If during MI_TECHNIQUES R escalates to Precontemplation (R > 0.75) ──► MI_TOPIC_EXPLORATION ──► MI_TECHNIQUES ──► (resume CBT stage)

MI Topic Exploration escape:
* Max 3 topics explored. If R still in Precontemplation ──► META_CONVERSATION (max 8 turns)
* If R enters Contemplation during meta-conversation ──► MI_TECHNIQUES
* If R still in Precontemplation after 8 meta-conversation turns ──► ANTICIPATED_CLOSING (session ends)

# AGENTS

The system is composed by two LLM-powered agents and an orchestrator:

## Therapist Agent
* **Role**: Conducts the therapy session following Beck's CBT methodology.
* **State access**: Full session state — cognitive model sets, technique evaluations, resistance predictions, reasoning traces, and conversation history.
* **Capabilities**: Generates therapeutic utterances, performs intermediate reasoning (resistance prediction, technique selection, cognitive model extraction), and produces structured JSON outputs for decision points.
* **System prompt**: Defined in `prompts/therapist_system.txt`.

## Patient Agent
* **Role**: Simulates a therapy patient with a specific profile, presenting problem, and resistance dynamics.
* **State access**: Patient profile (attitude, persona, presenting problem, reason for counseling, thinking patterns), current resistance value (R), and conversation history. Does NOT have access to therapist reasoning traces or session state internals.
* **Capabilities**: Generates patient utterances aligned with persona and attitude, performs resistance updates via reasoning step before each utterance.
* **System prompt**: Defined in `prompts/patient_system.txt`.

## Orchestrator (Environment)
* **Role**: The LangGraph graph itself. Manages stage transitions, conditional routing, prompt injection for intermediate reasoning steps, and state persistence.
* **Capabilities**: Controls turn-taking, injects stage-specific prompts before utterance generation, evaluates transition conditions, manages the `[/END]` token, and persists all reasoning traces.
* **Does NOT generate utterances**: All natural language is produced by the Therapist or Patient agents.

# TURN-TAKING PROTOCOL

Each conversation turn follows a strict sequence of steps orchestrated by the environment:

1. **Therapist reasoning chain**: The orchestrator injects stage-specific prompts (e.g., resistance prediction, transition checks, technique evaluation). The therapist agent produces structured outputs (JSON or text). These are saved as reasoning traces, NOT added to the conversation context.
2. **Therapist utterance**: Based on the reasoning outputs and conversation context, the therapist agent generates a natural language utterance. This IS added to the conversation context.
3. **Patient reasoning**: The patient agent receives the updated conversation and performs a resistance update step, producing a JSON with `r_value` and `reasoning`. This is saved as reasoning trace, NOT added to conversation context.
4. **Patient utterance**: The patient agent generates a natural language response aligned with their persona and updated resistance. This IS added to the conversation context.

The orchestrator then evaluates the updated state (resistance, stage conditions) and determines the next action before the cycle repeats.

# STAGES DETAILS
Specific objective, details and conditions to continue to next stage. The environment will have a state variable *current_stage*, to monitor on which session stage we are. 

## 1. Opening
**Objective**. Mood check and gather information from the patient. During this stage the therapist should extract and store: `opening_patient_mood` (a brief summary of the patient's expressed mood) and `opening_patient_problem` (a summary of the patient's stated problem). These variables will be used in later stages (e.g., Closing).

**Execution**. This stage is composed by a minimum of 6 utterances: First the therapist produces a brief welcome. The patient responds briefly. The therapist asks how it is going to evaluate mood check. The patient responds depending on their attitude. The therapist induces the patient to explain which is the patient's problem. Patient explains their problem. The execution will generate a varying number of utterances, depending on the number of patient utterances needed by the therapist to extract all the information required to delve into the patient's problem.

**Flow**. Next flow is followed:
1. Check if it is first utterance. If so, therapist generates a brief greeting to open the session. Patient responds. If not, continue.
2. Therapist checks if in previous patient utterance the patient has provided information about their mood or their presenting problem. If so, update saved mood/problem information and continue. If not, only continue.
3. Using therapist saved mood/problem, prompt the therapist to check if enough information was already collected to continue to next stage. If so, END stage. If not, specify what information is missing. If mood information should be refined, go to step 4. If problem information should be refined, go to step 5.
4. Therapist generates an utterance to gather information about patient mood. Patient responds. Then go to step 2
5. Therapist generates an utterance to gather information about patient problem. Patient responds. Then go to step 2

**End condition**. The therapist will be prompted: *"Based on the conversation so far, does the therapist have sufficient information about the patient's problem to proceed to the identification of cognitive distortions? Respond with JSON: `{"ready": true/false, "mood_summary": "<summary of patient mood>", "problem_summary": "<summary of patient problem>", "reasoning": "..."}`"*. If ready is true, store `opening_patient_mood` and `opening_patient_problem` from the response and the environment will continue to the next stage (determined by resistance — see State Transition Diagram). If ready is false, keep in current stage and continue extracting information from the patient.

**Fallback for uncooperative patients**. If after 10 exchange turns in Opening the therapist still cannot extract sufficient information, the environment should trigger MI Topic Exploration regardless of resistance level, to find an engagement angle.

## 2. Id. distortions
**Objective**. Delve into the patient's problem and identify cognitive distortions.
**Execution**. This stage is composed by a varying large number of utterances. During this stage the therapist should iteratively fill a reduced Beck's cognitive model that includes: Problematic Situations (situations), Automatic Thoughts (auto_thoughts), Emotions (emotions), Behaviors (behaviors) and Core Beliefs (core_beliefs).

**Cognitive model filling**. The items to be filled will be defined as empty array vars and the therapist will begin making questions and delving into the topic to extract the information in the next order: situations, auto_thoughts, emotions, behaviors. The therapist will be prompted *"Ask a question to extract a problematic situation."* and *"Ask a question to extract <item> associated to <situation>."* to iteratively extract the cognitive model items. After each patient utterance, the therapist will be prompted: *"Based on the patient's last utterance, which items from Beck's cognitive model can be extracted? Respond with JSON:"*
```json
{
    "situations": ["<s1>"],
    "auto_thoughts": ["<t1>"],
    "emotions": ["<e1>"],
    "behaviors": ["<b1>"]
}
```
This is done in case the patient has provided more information regarding the <situation> apart from the <item>. When merging this information with data in the already available cognitive model, auto_thoughts, emotions and behaviors should be associated with the situation currently being explored. Once a set of these four items (situation, auto_thoughts, emotions, behaviors) is complete, the core belief and distortion identification process begins (see below).

**Core belief identification**. Once a set of situation, auto_thoughts, emotions and behaviors is complete, the therapist will be prompted: *"Taking the cognitive model set (<situation>, <auto_thoughts>, <emotions>, <behaviors>), assign a probability to each core belief category. Respond with JSON:"*
```json
{
    "helplessness": 0.0,
    "unlovability": 0.0,
    "worthlessness": 0.0
}
```
Core belief categories are defined in <core_beliefs>. The framework selects the category with the highest probability as the assigned core belief for this cognitive model set.

**Distortion identification**. After core belief assignment, identify the cognitive distortion. The therapist will be prompted: *"Taking the cognitive model set (<situation>, <auto_thoughts>, <emotions>, <behaviors>, <core_belief>), assign a probability to each cognitive distortion. Respond with JSON:"*
```json
{
    "mental_filtering": 0.0,
    "fortune_telling": 0.0,
    "mind_reading": 0.0,
    "personalization": 0.0,
    "catastrophizing": 0.0,
    "all_or_nothing_thinking": 0.0,
    "should_statements": 0.0,
    "overgeneralization": 0.0,
    "labeling_and_mislabeling": 0.0,
    "discounting_the_positive": 0.0
}
```
Distortions are defined in <cognitive_distortions>. The distortion with the highest probability will be the one assigned to the cognitive model set. Once the distortion is assigned, we consider that we have a complete cognitive model set.

**Flow to fill the cognitive model**:
1. Identify a situation: Therapist asks about a specific situation that triggers the bad feelings.
2. Extraction of auto_thoughts, emotions and behaviors: Therapist continues making questions to extract these items one by one. It can happen that although the therapist asks one by one, the patient responds with more than one item. In this case the items that could be completed should be avoided to be asked again. All items will be associated with the current situation being explored.
3. From situation, auto_thoughts, emotions and behaviors, identify core belief (using probability assignment as described above).
4. From situation, auto_thoughts, emotions, behaviors and core_belief, identify cognitive distortion (using probability assignment as described above).
5. Return to step 1 to identify another situation. Stop when you consider you have enough information (at least 3 cognitive model sets of situation, auto_thoughts, emotions, behaviors, core_belief, distortion).

**Minimum cognitive model sets fallback**. If after 20 exchange turns in id_distortions only 2 cognitive model sets have been identified, the therapist may proceed with 2 sets, noting the limitation in reasoning traces. Hard floor: 2 complete cognitive model sets.

**End condition**. When the therapist has identified at least 3 cognitive model sets (or 2 after 20 turns), it will be prompted: *"Based on the cognitive model sets collected so far, does the therapist need more information about the patient's problem to proceed to technique application? Respond with JSON: `{"needs_more": true/false, "reasoning": "..."}`"*. If needs_more is false, the flow continues to cbt_technique stage. If true, continue extracting cognitive model sets (subject to the 20-turn fallback).

## 3. CBT Technique
**Objective**. Select and apply CBT techniques to address distortions identified.

### Terminology
* **Technique**: A specific CBT method from <cbt_techniques> (e.g., "Cognitive Restructuring", "Behavior Experiment").
* **Technique plan**: The therapist's specific plan for applying a given technique to the patient's case, including rationale, application steps, expected outcomes, and current progress.

**Execution**. This stage is composed by a varying large number of utterances. During this stage the therapist agent first selects a maximum of 3 techniques to address patient's distortions. Then, the agent begins applying the techniques one by one. For each technique, the therapist proposes a technique plan on how the technique is going to be applied. Then, the therapist will be prompted to begin creating utterances aligned with the technique, following the technique plan and the conversation context. The therapist will be prompted after each patient utterance if the technique application can be finished. If no, a new utterance following the technique plan is created. If yes, the therapist is prompted to evaluate the technique application, and then to re-evaluate whether the next planned technique is still the best fit given the current conversation status. When a minimum of three techniques are completed (independently of whether the conducted techniques were the ones initially conceived, or alternatives selected from re-evaluation) the therapist can be moved to the next stage.

**Technique completion criteria**. A technique is considered complete when: (1) All steps in the technique plan have been addressed in conversation, and (2) At least 4 therapist-patient exchange turns have occurred for this technique. Note that a technique can be considered completed even if the patient did not fully engage with it. **Hard safeguard**: If a technique has not been marked complete after 12 exchange turns, it is automatically considered complete and the flow proceeds to evaluation.

**Flow**. The next flow is used during this stage:
1. Therapist agent is prompted: *"Given the conversation context <context>, the cognitive model sets <cognitive_sets> and the distortions <distortions>, which techniques from <cbt_techniques> should be used to address the patient's problem? Select a maximum of 3. Respond with JSON: `{"selected_techniques": [{"name": "...", "target_distortion": "...", "rationale": "..."}], "reasoning": "..."}`"*
2. Therapist selects the next technique and creates the technique plan. The technique to develop is the next of the initially created list, or a re-planned technique if any technique was already applied. The agent is prompted: *"Given the technique <technique> to be applied, create a plan to effectively apply it to the specific patient case. The plan should include: Why the technique is useful for this case, How the technique is going to be applied to the specific case, expected outcomes, and current progress regarding the application of the technique. Respond with JSON: `{"technique_plan": "...", "steps": [...], "expected_outcomes": [...]}`"*. If this is not the first technique applied, include the evaluations from previously applied techniques as context to condition the planning.
3. Using the <technique_plan> and the <context>, the therapist creates an utterance aligned with the <technique>.
4. After patient utterance, the therapist is prompted: *"Based on the technique plan and the conversation, has the therapist completed all steps of the current technique application? (Minimum 4 exchange turns required.) Respond with JSON: `{"completed": true/false, "reasoning": "..."}`"*. If completed is true, continue to Step 5. If false, return to Step 3.
5. After finishing the technique, the therapist is prompted: *"Evaluate the technique application by answering: 1. Did the patient show resistance? 2. What were the results of the technique on the patient? 3. Were expected outcomes fulfilled? 4. If not fulfilled, explain the problems encountered and how they can be addressed in subsequent techniques. Respond with JSON."*
6. After finishing the technique evaluation, continue depending on the number of techniques completed:
    - If fewer than 3 completed: continue to Step 7.
    - If 3-4 completed: go to Step 6.1.
    - If 5 completed: finish stage.
    
    6.1. If technique count is 3-4, the therapist is prompted: *"Given the conversation context <context>, the cognitive model sets <cognitive_sets>, the distortions <distortions>, and the <technique_evaluations>, is it beneficial to apply any additional technique in today's session? Respond with JSON: `{"apply_more": true/false, "reasoning": "..."}`"*. If apply_more is true, continue to Step 7. If false, finish stage.
7. The therapist is prompted: *"The next planned technique is <next_technique>. Does it fit with the current conversation context and patient state? Respond with JSON: `{"fits": true/false, "reasoning": "...", "alternative_technique": "<name or null>", "alternative_reasoning": "..."}`"*. If fits is true, proceed to Step 2 with the planned technique. If false, proceed to Step 2 with the alternative technique. The therapist can replace a planned technique at most once per slot. If the replacement was also previously deemed unfit, proceed with the remaining original plan.

**End condition**. The therapist completed a minimum of 3 and a maximum of 5 techniques.

## 4. Homework
**Objective**. Assign homework to the patient for the next session.
**Execution**. Given the result of the techniques applied, the therapist proposes the patient to do some homework until the next session. The patient should give feedback about if they feel they can do the homework. This stage should continue until therapist and patient arrive to an agreement on the homework to do. Maximum of 3 homework proposal rounds.

**Flow**. Next flow is used during this stage:
1. Therapist is prompted: *"Based on the <cognitive_sets>, <distortions>, <technique_evaluations>, and <context>, propose tasks (minimum of 1, maximum of 2) to be done by the patient until the next session. Respond with JSON including for each: task_title, task_description, rationale, expected_outcomes."* If this is not the first tasks proposal trial, also include the feedback received by the patient to select new tasks.
2. Therapist creates an utterance based on the <tasks_reasoning> previously generated.
3. Patient responds and has to agree with the therapist to do at least one task. If the patient agrees on one task, continue the flow. If not, return to Step 1 to generate a new tasks set.
4. Patient responds and therapist is prompted: *"Has the therapist reached an agreement with the patient to do at least one task as homework? Respond with JSON: `{"agreement_reached": true/false, "agreed_tasks": [...], "reasoning": "..."}`"*. If agreement_reached is true, finish stage. If false, return to Step 1 to generate new tasks proposal with patient feedback.

**Maximum attempts safeguard**. If no agreement is reached after 3 proposal rounds, the therapist should simplify to the minimal possible task (e.g., a single observation or journaling task) and present it as a gentle suggestion rather than a firm assignment. Note in reasoning traces that full agreement was not reached.

**End condition**. Therapist-patient agreement to do at least one task as homework, or 3 proposal rounds exhausted (with simplified fallback task).

## 5. Closing
**Objective**. Summarize the session, next session plan and patient feedback.
**Execution**. First the therapist makes a brief summary of the complete session and checks if the patient has any doubt. Patient responds. Then the therapist proposes a tentative very concise plan for the next session. Patient responds with their opinion and therapist can refine plan until agreement. Finally the therapist asks for feedback about the session.

**Flow**. Next flow should be developed:
1. Using <opening_patient_mood>, <opening_patient_problem>, <cognitive_sets>, <distortions>, <technique_evaluations>, <tasks>, and <context>, create a brief summary of the complete session. It MUST be brief, not an overly long utterance with a lot of information. It should address only the most relevant session information, which should be kept in mind by the patient.
2. Patient responds, and therapist creates the next session plan based on <cognitive_sets>, <distortions>, <technique_evaluations>, <tasks>, and context.
3. Patient responds and they should agree with the plan. Prompt the therapist: *"Has the patient agreed with the next session plan? Respond with JSON: `{"agreed": true/false, "reasoning": "..."}`"*. If agreed is true, continue. If false, redo Step 3 applying patient feedback. Maximum of 3 refinement rounds for plan agreement; after that, proceed with the current plan.
4. After patient agreement, therapist should create an utterance where they ask the patient for feedback about the session.
5. Patient gives feedback and therapist is prompted: *"Did the patient report something the therapist can improve for the next session? Respond with JSON: `{"improvement_requested": true/false, "improvement_description": "...", "improvement_plan": "..."}`"*. If improvement was requested by the patient, generate improvement utterance based on <improvement_reasoning>, and then continue. If not, continue.
6. Patient responds and therapist generates an utterance to close the session. Finally the patient responds with another closing utterance. The orchestrator appends the special token [/END] to the patient's final utterance.

**[/END] token control**. The `[/END]` token is injected by the orchestrator/environment into the patient's final utterance after the closing exchange is complete (Step 6). The patient agent does not generate it; the environment appends it after the final patient response in the closing stage.

**End condition**: The orchestrator checks for the [/END] token in patient utterances.


# PATIENT RESISTANCE HANDLING

## Resistance Handling Engine
The implementation should include a strong mechanism for patient resistance, grounded on a continuous resistance variable contained in the patient, which measures their openness and predisposition to take the therapy. This is grounded on the TTM and we put our focus on states Precontemplation, Contemplation and Action. Preparation is omitted as is the planning state prior to begin the action, in this work we consider preparation+action as the unified state of Action, as in preparation the patient is already convinced to make changes. We model the TTM with continuous values as next:
* Precontemplation: R in (1-0.75]
* Contemplation: R in (0.75-0.5]
* Action: R<0.5
As patients are initialized with three different attitudes (Positive, Neutral and Negative), next values will be init to patients depending on their attitude:
* Negative: R=0.75
* Neutral: R=0.55
* Positive: R=0.35

Prior to each therapist utterance, this agent will be prompted to predict the current resistance of the patient. If therapist is not in OPENING stage, the next actions will be done depending on resistance:
* Contemplation R: Execute MI techniques (to get the patient into the therapy)
* Action R: Execute CBT flow (to treat the patient's problem)
If the therapist is in the OPENING stage the flow is different. During this stage the therapist will monitor the resistance but it will only decide the path after OPENING is completed. In this moment next decisions can be done:
* Precontemplation R: Execute MI topic exploration module. Described in MI subsection
* Contemplation R: Execute MI techniques (to get the patient into the therapy). Described in MI techniques subsection
* Action R: Execute CBT flow (to treat the patient's problem)

### Resistance prediction vs. actual resistance
The therapist *predicts* resistance, but the patient *has* actual resistance. The therapist's resistance prediction is used for *therapist-side decision-making* (which flow to follow). The patient's *actual* resistance determines patient behavior. Misalignment between predicted and actual resistance is intentional and produces realistic miscommunication. The environment uses the **therapist's predicted resistance** for routing decisions, since in a real scenario the therapist does not have access to the patient's internal state.

After each therapist utterance, patient resistance changes depending on the alignment of the utterance with the problem and how the patient felt that utterance helped them to improve their situation. The change can be 0 if the patient considered that no change in resistance was produced.

## CBT Flow Interruption
CBT stages for the purpose of MI interruption include: `id_distortions`, `cbt_technique`, and `homework`. All three can be interrupted by MI if resistance enters Contemplation range.

If the therapist is in a CBT stage (not opening) and inferred patient state is in Contemplation interval, the CBT should be interrupted to apply MI techniques, which are more targeted on minimizing resistance. When resistance enters the Contemplation interval the next flow happens:
1. Save CBT flow state. Therapist should save at which point the flow was interrupted, this can be done through a summary of the status.
2. Apply MI techniques. Move the focus to apply MI techniques.
3. **Escalation**: During MI techniques application, if resistance escalates to Precontemplation (R > 0.75), escalate to MI Topic Exploration. After topic exploration brings R back to Contemplation, return to MI Techniques.
4. When resistance goes back into the Action state, next actions should be performed:
    4.1. Using <mi_context> (includes only utterances produced during MI flow), prompt the therapist: *"Create an evaluation of the MI intervention. Respond with JSON: `{"reason_for_mi": "...", "conclusions": "...", "patient_state_change": "..."}`"*
    4.2. Re-attach CBT flow using the saved summary of Step 1.

### Resistance monitoring during Homework and Closing
* **During Homework**: Resistance is monitored. If resistance enters Contemplation range, apply MI techniques before continuing homework negotiation.
* **During Closing**: Resistance is monitored but **not acted upon**. Any resistance observations are logged in reasoning traces for the next session.

## Motivational Interviewing (MI) Module
If patient is in Precontemplation/Contemplation states, the therapist should use MI techniques to prepare the patient to get into CBT. Next MI submodules will be described.

### MI Topic Exploration
If the patient is in Precontemplation state, the therapist should find a proper motivation to produce a change on the patient. The therapist should navigate through different topics to find a motivation to change patient's behavior. Following CAMI paper (ACL 2025) we will use topics defined in <mi_topics>.

**Flow**. Therapist decides which is the best topic to address and creates utterance including how their current problem affects the topic selected. Patient responds and updates their resistance. Therapist infers resistance based on patient response; if resistance is higher with respect to the previous utterance, change topic; if it is lower, delve into the topic until resistance is in Contemplation interval.

**Escape condition**. Maximum of 3 topic changes. If after exploring 3 different topics resistance has not entered the Contemplation interval, the session enters the meta-conversation flow.

#### Meta-Conversation Flow
When MI Topic Exploration has exhausted 3 topics without reducing resistance to Contemplation, the therapist shifts to a meta-level discussion about the therapy process itself:

1. **Acknowledge difficulty**. The therapist is prompted: *"The patient remains resistant after exploring multiple motivational topics. Generate an empathic utterance that acknowledges the patient's difficulty with the therapy process and opens a meta-level conversation about what would make this process more comfortable or useful for them."* Therapist generates utterance.
2. Patient responds. Resistance updates.
3. **Explore barriers**. The therapist is prompted: *"Based on the patient's response about the therapy process, explore what specific barriers or concerns prevent them from engaging. Use empathic listening and validate their perspective."* Therapist generates utterance.
4. Patient responds. Resistance updates. If R enters Contemplation → exit to MI Techniques.
5. **Address barriers collaboratively**. The therapist is prompted: *"Based on the barriers identified, propose a collaborative adjustment to the therapy approach that respects the patient's concerns while maintaining therapeutic value."* Therapist generates utterance.
6. Patient responds. Resistance updates. If R enters Contemplation → exit to MI Techniques.
7. Continue dialoguing with empathic, non-judgmental utterances until either R enters Contemplation or 8 total meta-conversation exchange turns are reached.
8. **Anticipated closing**. If after 8 meta-conversation turns R has not decreased to Contemplation, the session ends with anticipated closing:
    - Therapist generates a warm, non-judgmental closing utterance that validates the patient's attendance and leaves the door open for future sessions.
    - Patient responds with a closing utterance.
    - The orchestrator appends [/END] to the patient's final utterance. Session ends.

### MI Techniques
When patient resistance is in Contemplation interval, the therapist should apply some techniques to continue lowering their resistance. MI techniques that can be used are defined in <mi_techniques>.

**Flow**. Therapist selects a technique, and generates an utterance according to the technique. Patient responds. If resistance is lower with respect to the previous utterance, continue applying that technique or a similar one. If it is higher, try another appropriate technique. Do this iteratively until the patient resistance is within the Action interval. At that moment, CBT flow can be executed or resumed.

**Escalation to Topic Exploration**. During MI Techniques application, resistance must continue to be monitored. If resistance escalates to Precontemplation (R > 0.75), the flow immediately transitions to MI Topic Exploration.

**Escape condition**. Maximum of 10 exchange turns in MI Techniques. If after 10 turns resistance has not entered Action range, proceed to CBT anyway with a note in reasoning traces that MI did not fully resolve resistance.

## Patient Reasoning
The patient should reason before producing their utterance. In this reasoning the patient should infer the update that should be applied to its Resistance variable and a reasoning on how the therapist utterance affected them. This reasoning done prior to utterance is obtained prompting the patient: *"Given the <context>, the current resistance value <current_resistance> to engage in treatment, and the patient's <personal_info> (including presenting_problem, reason_for_counseling, thinking_patterns, etc.), how much did the therapist's last utterance help address the patient's problems? Respond with JSON: `{"r_value": <float>, "reasoning": "..."}`. The resistance value change per-turn is constrained to be in [-0.10, +0.15]."*

Note that it is easier to increase resistance than decrease it. Higher values of resistance mean that the patient is less open to engage in treatment.


# REASONING TRACES

As indicated during this system description, when executing the different stages some prompts are done before generating the actual utterances that will be added to the conversation context. All these intermediate outputs should be saved as reasoning traces of the specific utterance saved. For each intermediate, save as reasoning trace the input-output pair. If more than one intermediate is created, compose a string with input-output intermediate pairs. Each reasoning trace should be clearly associated to its reference utterance.

**Reasoning trace schema**:
```json
{
    "utterance_index": 12,
    "speaker": "therapist",
    "traces": [
        {
            "step": "resistance_prediction",
            "input": "Based on the patient's last response...",
            "output": "Predicted resistance: 0.62 (Contemplation)"
        },
        {
            "step": "technique_completion_check",
            "input": "Has the therapist completed all steps of the current technique?",
            "output": "{\"completed\": false, \"reasoning\": \"The patient has not yet...\"}"
        }
    ]
}
```

For patient utterances, the reasoning trace captures the resistance update:
```json
{
    "utterance_index": 13,
    "speaker": "patient",
    "traces": [
        {
            "step": "resistance_update",
            "input": "Given the context, current R=0.62...",
            "output": "{\"r_value\": 0.55, \"reasoning\": \"The therapist's reflection helped me feel understood...\"}"
        }
    ]
}
```

# SYSTEM PROMPTS

Agent system prompts are defined in separate files:
* **Therapist agent**: `prompts/therapist_system.txt` — Contains CBT knowledge base, dialogue rules, and the therapist persona definition.
* **Patient agent**: `prompts/patient_system.txt` — Contains persona placeholder, thinking patterns, attitude configuration, and dialogue rules.

Both prompts use template variables (e.g., `{conversation}`, `{persona}`, `{attitude}`) that are filled by the orchestrator at runtime with the current session state values.
